Bookkeeping Clerk Career Assessment

Recording the money coming in and going out, and making sure it adds up

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

A business sells something, pays a bill, and buys supplies. Someone has to record each of those and make sure it all adds up. That is the bookkeeper's job.

This career is about numbers, order, and careful checking. Explore what it feels like before deciding whether it interests you.

Go at your pace. The short questions at the end are for reflection. There are no right or wrong answers.

2What is a Bookkeeping Clerk?

You keep a business's money records. You enter sales and bills into software, match receipts and bank statements, and find mistakes. Your records help owners and accountants make good decisions.

  • Record money coming in and going out.
  • Enter bills and invoices and track which are paid.
  • Match bank statements to the business's records.
  • Find and fix mistakes when numbers do not match.
  • Prepare simple reports for owners or accountants.
  • Keep financial files organized.

The real picture: In a small business, one bookkeeper may do everything. In a larger company, you might handle just one part, like bills or customer payments.

3A Day in the Life

One possible day: The work follows the calendar.

  • Morning: Enter yesterday's sales and receipts into the bookkeeping software.
  • Later: Pay bills that are due this week and file the invoices.
  • Midday: Answer a question from a vendor about a payment.
  • Afternoon: Match the bank statement to the records and track down a small difference.
  • Before leaving: Note what is left before the end-of-month report.

The end of the month and tax time are often the busiest. Many bookkeepers like the quiet focus of the rest of the month.

4How You Learn the Job

There is more than one way in. About 4 in 10 people in this job say a high school diploma or GED is what new workers need. About 1 in 5 say a short college certificate. Small businesses often train on the job. Read the actual job ad.

  • Build basic skills first: Spreadsheets, careful data entry, and basic math with percentages.
  • Learn the software: Many employers use QuickBooks or similar programs.
  • Optional education: North Dakota schools offering accounting technology or bookkeeping certificates and associate degrees include Williston State College, Dickinson State University, Bismarck State College, Turtle Mountain College in Belcourt, Dakota College at Bottineau, Minot State University, and Rasmussen University in Fargo.
  • On-the-job training: Each business has its own way of organizing its books.

Paying for training:

  1. Start with financial aid, scholarships, and grants. The school's financial aid office can help.
  2. Ask employers whether they help pay for training.
  3. Ask for the full cost, including books, fees, travel, and a computer.
  4. Ask your career counselor about other help, such as WIOA. WIOA is a federal program that can help some people pay for job training. Ask before you enroll.

Employer help with school may come with a promise to keep working there. Read the terms first.

5Training and Employer Requirements

No state license is needed to be a bookkeeper. Requirements depend on the employer. Ask what you need to start.

  • Comfort with spreadsheets and numbers.
  • Experience with bookkeeping software, or willingness to learn it.
  • A background check for some jobs.
  • Care with private financial information.
  • Optional bookkeeping certifications; ask whether an employer values one before you pay for it.

Before you begin: Ask who reviews your work at first and who you go to when numbers do not match.

Ask about written instructions, an accessible workstation, extra practice time, or other support that helps you learn and do the work well.

6Pay and Job Outlook

North Dakota bookkeeping clerk pay — May 2025:

  • Middle pay: $23.90 an hour ($49,710 a year). Half of workers earned more and half earned less.
  • Half of workers earned between $21.52 and $28.36 an hour.
  • 1 in 10 earned less than $17.95 an hour, and 1 in 10 earned more than $31.75.
  • These numbers show what workers earned. They are not a promise of what a new worker will start at.
  • North Dakota had about 4,440 of these jobs in 2024. The state projects about 4,330 in 2034, a change of -3%. That works out to about 490 openings a year, mostly from people who retire or change jobs.

Where people work: Small businesses, farms and ranches, accounting offices, clinics, schools, nonprofits, and government offices.

Work setting: An office desk with a computer. Some jobs are part time or can be partly remote once you are trained.

Compare an offer: Ask about base pay, hours, busy seasons, benefits, paid time off, training, and help paying for classes.

7Where This Can Lead

  • Full-charge bookkeeper: Handle all of a small business's books.
  • Payroll: Specialize in paying employees correctly.
  • Accountant: A bachelor's degree in accounting can lead to accountant jobs.
  • Office manager: Combine bookkeeping with running the office.

Some bookkeepers work for themselves and keep books for several small businesses.

8The Real Talk

Things you might like:

  • Work where things add up and you can check them.
  • Quiet, focused tasks.
  • Jobs in many kinds of businesses, including farms and ranches.
  • A path toward accounting.

Things to ask about:

  • Long stretches at a computer.
  • Busy deadlines at month end and tax time.
  • Hunting for a small mistake that throws everything off.
  • Changes in software and how the work is done.

North Dakota expects a small drop in these jobs over ten years, with about 490 openings a year, mostly from retirements and job changes.

A question for yourself: Do you enjoy making things add up and finding what is wrong when they do not?
