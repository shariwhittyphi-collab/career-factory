Computer Systems Analyst Career Assessment

Connecting business needs and technology — ND in-demand occupation

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Computer systems analysts figure out how technology can help an organization work better.

You will explore this "bridge" role between business and IT and whether it fits you.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Computer Systems Analyst?

A systems analyst studies how an organization works, finds problems, and recommends technology solutions. They gather requirements, work with developers, test new systems, and train users.

The Reality: You spend a lot of time talking with people, not just computers. You must understand both business and tech, and explain each to the other side.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Interview users about their needs
  • Study how work gets done now
  • Recommend new systems or changes
  • Write requirements for developers
  • Test new systems
  • Train users
  • Estimate costs and benefits
  • Fix problems after launch

You translate between business and technology.

3A Day in the Life

8:30 AM — Meet with the billing team about problems with their system.

10:00 AM — Map out their current process.

11:30 AM — Write requirements for a fix.

12:30 PM — Lunch.

1:30 PM — Meet developers to review the plan.

3:00 PM — Test a new feature.

4:00 PM — Write training notes for users.

People-focused problem solving with technology.

4Training in North Dakota

Usually a bachelor's degree. Computer information systems (CIS) or business degrees are common. No prior experience is required for many entry jobs.

Training Pathways:

  • Bachelor's in CIS or Information Systems: The most direct path.
  • Business degree + IT skills: Also works.
  • MBA (optional): For senior roles.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

  • University of North Dakota (Grand Forks or online) — B.S. Computer Science; BBA Information Systems
  • North Dakota State University (Fargo) — B.S. Computer Science (tracks incl. Data Science, Software Engineering, Cybersecurity); Information Technology major
  • Minot State University (Minot or online) — B.S. Computer Information Systems; B.S. Computer Science
  • Valley City State University (Valley City or online) — B.S. Computer Information Systems; Software Engineering
  • Dickinson State University (Dickinson, Bismarck, or online) — B.S. Computer Science; B.S. Computer Technology Management
  • University of Mary (Bismarck) — B.S. Computer Information Systems; B.S. Computer Science
  • Valley City State University — Business Process Integration Management

Before You Start: Strong communication and problem-solving. A bachelor's degree is typical.

Tip: Start with financial aid, scholarships, and any help an employer offers — that is where the money starts. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like training, exam fees, or a laptop. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you sign up rather than after. Many IT certifications are also sold as low-cost online courses you can do at home.

5Certification & Licensing

No license. Certifications help. Business analysis certifications are valued.

Certifications Employers Look For:

  • CBAP (Certified Business Analysis Professional) — needs 5+ years of experience
  • Entry-level business analysis certificates

The Exams: Computer-based.

Cost: CBAP: about $145 application plus $350–$505 exam. Prices change.

Keeping It Current: Continuing education required.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Computer Systems Analyst Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $37.39–$42.05/hour ($77,770–$87,470/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $50.71/hour ($105,470/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $53.49–$62.85/hour ($111,250–$130,730/year)

What that means: Very high pay in ND, even at the starting level.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 210 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 220 (about 6% growth). Expect about 10 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is about 9% growth — much faster than average.

Outlook: Growing nationally. ND lists about 10 openings a year, but many similar jobs go by titles like "business analyst" or "IT analyst."

Who Hires in ND: State government (NDIT in Bismarck), Microsoft's Fargo campus, banks and insurance companies, health systems, energy companies, universities, and IT service companies. Many remote jobs exist.

Work Setting: Offices, with remote options. Regular hours.

Physical Demands: Light. Desk work and meetings.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 15-1211); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Senior Analyst — Lead bigger projects.

Path 2: Business Analyst — Focus on business needs.

Path 3: IT Project Manager — Run projects.

Path 4: Solutions Architect — Design whole systems.

Path 5: IT Manager — Lead teams.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • High pay
  • Mix of people and tech
  • No coding required in many roles
  • Growing nationally
  • Remote options

What's Hard:

  • Lots of meetings
  • Balancing different groups' needs
  • Usually need a degree
  • Blame when projects slip
  • Few ND openings listed

Be Honest: Do you enjoy talking with people and solving problems? Can you learn both business and tech? Are you ready for a bachelor's degree?
