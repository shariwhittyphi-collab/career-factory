Community Health Worker Career Assessment

Helping neighbors understand their health and find care

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Sometimes the most helpful health worker is someone from the neighborhood who knows how to explain things and where to find help.

This job mixes outreach, education, and support. Explore what it feels like before deciding whether it interests you.

Go at your pace. The short questions at the end are for reflection. There are no right or wrong answers.

2What is a Community Health Worker?

You help people understand their health and connect with care and services. You often work in the community you come from.

  • Visit people at home or meet them in the community.
  • Explain health information in plain words.
  • Help people manage conditions like diabetes day to day.
  • Connect people with clinics and services.
  • Follow up to see how things are going.
  • Keep records of visits.

The real picture: Titles vary, such as community health representative, outreach worker, or patient navigator.

3A Day in the Life

One possible day: Every program works differently.

  • Morning: Plan home visits and check messages.
  • Later: Visit a client to talk about managing blood sugar.
  • Midday: Help another client schedule a clinic visit and find a ride.
  • Afternoon: Staff a table at a community event.
  • Before leaving: Record visits and plan follow-ups.

Much of the day is spent on the road and with people, not at a desk.

4How You Learn the Job

North Dakota began certifying community health workers in October 2025. Certification creates a way for North Dakota Medicaid to pay for their services.

  • Certification pathways: A CHW training program plus a 200-hour internship; Indian Health Service community health representative training; 1,000 hours of supervised CHW work within three years; or certification from another state.
  • College: Some employers want a degree in public health or a related field. North Dakota options include UND, NDSU, Dickinson State University, the University of Jamestown, and Turtle Mountain College.
  • On-the-job training: Employers teach their programs and community resources.

Paying for training:

  1. Start with financial aid, scholarships, and grants. The school's financial aid office can help.
  2. Ask employers whether they help pay for training.
  3. Ask for the full cost, including books, fees, travel, and a computer.
  4. Ask your career counselor about other help, such as WIOA. WIOA is a federal program that can help some people pay for job training. Ask before you enroll.

Employer help with school may come with a promise to keep working there. Read the terms first.

5Training and Employer Requirements

Ask each employer whether they require North Dakota CHW certification. Certified CHWs renew with 12 hours of continuing education, including 2 on legal and ethical topics.

  • Strong ties to the community you will serve.
  • A driver’s license and reliable car for visits.
  • A background check.
  • Comfort keeping health information private.

Before you begin: Ask how the employer trains and supervises CHWs.

Ask about written instructions, an accessible workstation, extra practice time, or other support that helps you learn and do the work well.

6Pay and Job Outlook

North Dakota community health worker pay — May 2025:

  • Middle pay: $28.66 an hour ($59,610 a year). Half of workers earned more and half earned less.
  • Half of workers earned between $22.18 and $35.33 an hour.
  • 1 in 10 earned less than $21.54 an hour, and 1 in 10 earned more than $36.64.
  • These numbers show what workers earned. They are not a promise of what a new worker will start at.
  • North Dakota had about 90 of these jobs in 2024. The state projects about 100 in 2034, a change of +10%. That works out to about 10 openings a year, mostly from people who retire or change jobs.

Where people work: Public health units, clinics and hospitals, tribal health programs, and nonprofits.

Work setting: Homes, community places, and offices, with a lot of driving.

Compare an offer: Ask about base pay, mileage, whether the job is grant funded, benefits, and training.

7Where This Can Lead

  • Senior CHW or team lead: Train and support new CHWs.
  • Health educator: With more education.
  • Nursing or social work: Many CHWs go on to college.
  • Program coordinator: Run a community health program.

Ask people in public health near you which paths are open.

8The Real Talk

Things you might like:

  • Helping your own community.
  • Time out with people instead of at a desk.
  • A growing field with new certification.
  • Seeing people get healthier.

Things to ask about:

  • Few jobs in some areas.
  • Travel and mileage.
  • Jobs that depend on grant funding.
  • The emotional weight of people’s struggles.

North Dakota projects about 10% growth in these jobs from 2024 to 2034. It is a small field, with about 10 openings a year.

A question for yourself: Would you like to be the trusted person who helps neighbors find care?
