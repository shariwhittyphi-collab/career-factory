Sheet Metal Worker Career Assessment

Building and installing ductwork, roofs, and metal parts for buildings

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

The ducts that carry warm air through a school in January were built and installed by sheet metal workers.

This trade mixes math, metal, and building. Explore what it feels like before deciding whether it interests you.

Go at your pace. The short questions at the end are for reflection. There are no right or wrong answers.

2What is a Sheet Metal Worker?

You make and install products from thin metal, such as heating and cooling ducts, roof flashing, and gutters.

  • Read drawings and measure carefully.
  • Cut, bend, and shape metal in a shop.
  • Join seams by riveting, welding, or soldering.
  • Install ducts in buildings, often from lifts.
  • Install metal roofing and flashing.
  • Repair and maintain systems.

The real picture: Some days are in a fabrication shop. Others are on a construction site.

3A Day in the Life

One possible day: Every job site is different.

  • Morning: Review the drawings with the crew.
  • Later: Cut and bend sheet metal into duct sections in the shop.
  • Midday: Load sections and take them to the job site.
  • Afternoon: Hang and fasten ductwork from a scissor lift.
  • Before leaving: Clean up scraps and mark tomorrow’s start point.

Apprentices also spend some evenings or blocks of time in class.

4How You Learn the Job

Most sheet metal workers learn through an apprenticeship. You work for pay during the day and take classes.

  • North and South Dakota apprenticeship: The Sheet Metal Workers’ joint apprenticeship program has training in Bismarck and Minot and in Fargo and Grand Forks. Applicants must be at least 18 with a high school diploma or GED. Applications are taken year round.
  • Pay while learning: Apprentice pay rises in steps as you finish work hours, until you reach full journeyworker pay.
  • Other routes: Some HVAC contractors train helpers on the job.

Paying for training: In an apprenticeship you are paid while you learn, and pay usually rises as you complete hours. For classes or a certificate, start with financial aid, scholarships, and grants. Ask employers whether they help pay. Ask your career counselor about other help, such as WIOA, a federal program that can help some people pay for job training. Ask before you enroll.

5Training and Employer Requirements

North Dakota does not have a separate state license for sheet metal workers. Contractors need a state contractor license. Ask employers what they require.

  • Age 18 or older and a high school diploma or GED for the apprenticeship.
  • Basic math and measuring skills.
  • Ability to lift, climb, and work on lifts, or ask about the physical demands.
  • A driver’s license for travel to job sites.

Before you begin: Ask how apprentices are supported in class and on the job.

Ask about written instructions, a buddy on your first jobs, extra practice time, or other support that helps you learn and work safely.

6Pay and Job Outlook

North Dakota sheet metal worker pay — May 2025:

  • Middle pay: $31.34 an hour ($65,180 a year). Half of workers earned more and half earned less.
  • Half of workers earned between $23.51 and $38.91 an hour.
  • 1 in 10 earned less than $23.11 an hour, and 1 in 10 earned more than $40.74.
  • These numbers show what workers earned. They are not a promise of what a new worker will start at.
  • North Dakota had about 390 of these jobs in 2024. The state projects about 420 in 2034, a change of +6%. That works out to about 30 openings a year, mostly from people who retire or change jobs.

Where people work: HVAC and sheet metal contractors, roofing contractors, and manufacturers.

Work setting: Fabrication shops and construction sites, sometimes on lifts. Mostly full time.

Compare an offer: Ask about apprentice pay steps, overtime, benefits, and class schedules.

7Where This Can Lead

  • Journeyworker: Finish the apprenticeship and earn full trade pay.
  • Foreman: Lead crews on big projects.
  • Specialist: HVAC testing and balancing, architectural metal, or welding.
  • Estimator or project manager: Plan jobs with experience.

Ask people in the trade which paths are open.

8The Real Talk

Things you might like:

  • Paid training instead of school debt.
  • Building real things from flat metal.
  • Strong pay for experienced workers.
  • A mix of shop and job-site work.

Things to ask about:

  • Sharp edges, noise, and lifting.
  • Heights and lifts.
  • Travel to job sites.
  • Several years of apprenticeship before full pay.

North Dakota projects about 6% growth in these jobs from 2024 to 2034, with about 30 openings a year.

A question for yourself: Does building with metal while you earn and learn sound good to you?
