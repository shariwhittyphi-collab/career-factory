Computer Programmer Career Assessment

Writing and testing code — ND in-demand occupation

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Computer programmers write and test the code that makes software work.

You will explore what this job is really like, including how it is changing, and whether it fits you.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Computer Programmer?

A programmer turns software designs into working code. They write code, test it, fix bugs, and update older programs. Programmers usually work from plans made by software developers or analysts.

The Reality: This job is shrinking nationally because AI tools and developers now do much of the routine coding. Programmers who also design software (developers) have better prospects.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Write code in languages like Python, Java, C#, or SQL
  • Test and debug programs
  • Update older software
  • Use AI coding tools and check their work
  • Follow design plans
  • Write documentation
  • Work with developers and testers

You turn plans into working programs.

3A Day in the Life

8:30 AM — Check messages and the task list.

9:00 AM — Write code for a new report screen.

11:00 AM — Fix a bug a tester found.

12:00 PM — Lunch.

1:00 PM — Update an older program for a new tax rule.

3:00 PM — Code review with a teammate.

4:30 PM — Write notes.

Focused coding work at a computer.

4Training in North Dakota

Usually a bachelor's degree. Most programmers have a computer science or related degree. Internships help a lot.

Training Pathways:

  • Bachelor's degree (4 years): Computer science or software engineering.
  • Associate degree: Can lead to some entry jobs.
  • Portfolio: Show projects you built.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

  • University of North Dakota (Grand Forks or online) — B.S. Computer Science; BBA Information Systems
  • North Dakota State University (Fargo) — B.S. Computer Science (tracks incl. Data Science, Software Engineering, Cybersecurity); Information Technology major
  • Minot State University (Minot or online) — B.S. Computer Information Systems; B.S. Computer Science
  • Valley City State University (Valley City or online) — B.S. Computer Information Systems; Software Engineering
  • Dickinson State University (Dickinson, Bismarck, or online) — B.S. Computer Science; B.S. Computer Technology Management
  • University of Mary (Bismarck) — B.S. Computer Information Systems; B.S. Computer Science
  • ND State College of Science (Fargo or online) — AAS Computer Science, Software Engineering emphasis

Before You Start: Strong logic and math. Internships are preferred by employers.

Tip: Start with financial aid, scholarships, and any help an employer offers — that is where the money starts. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like training, exam fees, or a laptop. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you sign up rather than after. Many IT certifications are also sold as low-cost online courses you can do at home.

5Certification & Licensing

No license needed. Your code and portfolio matter more than certifications.

Certifications Employers Look For:

  • A portfolio of projects
  • Language or cloud certificates — optional

The Exams: Job interviews often include coding tests.

Cost: Varies.

Keeping It Current: Keep your skills current.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Computer Programmer Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $28.62–$37.10/hour ($59,530–$77,180/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $44.37/hour ($92,280/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $54.78–$66.81/hour ($113,940–$138,960/year)

What that means: Good pay in ND, close to national levels.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 240 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 230 (about 6% fewer jobs). Expect about 10 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is about 6% fewer jobs — decline.

Outlook: Declining. Only about 10 openings a year in ND. Consider software developer or QA paths, which are growing.

Who Hires in ND: State government (NDIT in Bismarck), Microsoft's Fargo campus, banks and insurance companies, health systems, energy companies, universities, and IT service companies. Many remote jobs exist.

Work Setting: Offices or remote. Regular hours.

Physical Demands: Light. Long hours at a screen.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 15-1251); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Software Developer — Design and build software (growing).

Path 2: QA Automation Engineer — Write tests.

Path 3: Data Engineer — Build data systems.

Path 4: Web Developer — Build websites.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • Good pay
  • Remote options
  • Logic and problem-solving
  • Skills transfer to growing jobs

What's Hard:

  • Shrinking job
  • AI is taking over routine coding
  • Usually need a degree
  • Long screen time
  • Few ND openings

Be Honest: Are you ready to grow into software development, not just programming? Will you keep learning as AI changes the work?
