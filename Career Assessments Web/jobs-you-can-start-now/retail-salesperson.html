Retail Salesperson Career Assessment

On the floor, where the pay is better than the register

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Retail salespeople work the floor — helping customers choose, knowing the products, keeping stock full and displays right.

About 1,570 positions open in North Dakota each year, and it pays about $2.20 an hour more than cashiering.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Retail Salesperson?

You help people work out what they need and whether it is worth the money. That means actually knowing the products — which boot lasts, which tool is over-specified for the job, what fits. In between customers you stock, face shelves, price, and set displays. In some stores part of your pay depends on what you sell.

The Reality: You are on your feet and moving for the whole shift, which is easier on the body than standing still but harder on the mileage. Some customers want twenty minutes of your expertise and then buy it online in the car park. If you are on commission, a slow week is a smaller cheque. Evenings, weekends, and the whole of December.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Learn the products well enough to advise, not just point
  • Help customers decide what actually fits their need
  • Stock, face, and price the shelves
  • Set and maintain displays
  • Handle returns and complaints on the floor
  • Ring up sales when the front end is busy
  • Watch for theft without accusing anyone
  • Keep working through quiet stretches without being told

The good ones stop people buying the wrong thing, and get thanked for it.

3A Day in the Life

9:00 AM — Clock in. Walk the department and see what sold overnight.

9:30 AM — Unload and put out a delivery.

10:30 AM — First customer of the day needs help choosing between two products.

12:00 PM — Busy stretch. Four customers at once, all with questions.

1:00 PM — Break.

1:30 PM — Reset an end display for a promotion starting tomorrow.

3:00 PM — A return that is outside policy. Sort it without losing the customer.

5:00 PM — Face the shelves, tidy the department, hand over.

A mix of helping people and keeping the department right.

4Training in North Dakota

No school needed. You learn the products on the job. Employers train you on the systems in a few days. Product knowledge takes longer and is the part that makes you good — most of it comes from paying attention and asking.

Training Pathways:

  • Walk-in or online application: Retail hires year-round, heavily from October for the holidays.
  • Move over from the front end: Cashiers who know the store are exactly who gets asked to move to the floor.
  • Specialty stores pay for knowledge: Hardware, farm and ranch, auto parts, and sporting goods pay more for people who genuinely know the product — and a hobby counts as knowledge.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

Before You Start: No diploma or experience required at most employers. You need to be able to stand and walk for a full shift and lift stock. Specialty stores will care more about what you know than about your resume.

Tip: Most of these jobs need no school, so the cost of starting is not tuition — it is the things you need in the first month. Non-slip shoes, work clothes or a uniform, a way to get to work, and getting through the two or three weeks before the first paycheck arrives. Ask the employer first: many will supply a uniform, some give a shoe allowance, and some pay weekly rather than every two weeks, which matters a lot at the start. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like work clothes, boots, tools, or getting to work. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you start rather than after.

5Certification & Licensing

No license or certificate is required. Nothing official is needed. Some specialty departments have manufacturer product training, which the employer arranges and pays for.

Worth asking about:

  • Manufacturer product training — tools, appliances, paint, tyres; free, employer-arranged, and makes you more valuable
  • Forklift certification — useful in farm, ranch, and building supply stores; the employer provides it
  • Loss prevention training — how to handle suspected theft safely and lawfully

The Exams: None required to start work.

Cost: Nothing. Employer-arranged product training is free to you.

Keeping It Current: Forklift certification is usually renewed every three years.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Retail Salesperson Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $13.53–$14.59/hour ($28,140–$30,340/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $17.33/hour ($36,040/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $18.83–$23.95/hour ($39,160–$49,820/year)

What that means: The top of this range is specialty retail — farm and ranch, building supply, auto parts, appliances — where product knowledge is worth real money, and commission departments. General retail sits nearer the middle. Ask whether pay includes commission and what a normal month looks like, not a good one.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 10,820 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 11,040 (about 2% growth). Expect about 1570 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is little or no change.

Outlook: Steady. About 1,570 openings a year in North Dakota, and the total is holding roughly flat. Specialty retail is the more durable end — it is harder to replace someone who actually knows the product.

Who Hires in ND: Farm and ranch supply, building supply and hardware, auto parts, sporting goods, furniture and appliances, clothing, and big-box retail.

Work Setting: Indoors on a sales floor, on your feet and moving. Evenings, weekends, and heavy hours from October through December.

Physical Demands: Moderate to high. Standing and walking the whole shift, lifting and carrying stock, reaching and climbing small ladders.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 41-2031); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Sales Associate — Where most people start.

Path 2: Department Lead — Run a department, order stock, train new staff.

Path 3: Specialty Sales — Farm, building supply, auto parts — knowledge pays.

Path 4: Assistant Manager — Scheduling, staffing, targets.

Path 5: Store Manager — Run the store. Salaried.

Path 6: Outside Sales or Buyer — Sell to businesses, or choose what the store carries.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • Pays about $2.20 an hour more than cashiering
  • No school or experience needed
  • Being genuinely knowledgeable is rewarded
  • Moving around beats standing still
  • Specialty stores reward a hobby you already have
  • Clear ladder into management

What's Hard:

  • Evenings, weekends, and a punishing December
  • On your feet the entire shift
  • People use your advice and then buy online
  • Commission means slow weeks pay less
  • Sales targets in some stores
  • Difficult customers are yours to handle on the floor

Be Honest: Do you enjoy knowing things well enough to explain them simply? Can you stay helpful with someone who will not buy? Are you all right with evenings, weekends, and December?
