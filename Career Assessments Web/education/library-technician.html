Library Technician Career Assessment

Helping people find books, information, and computers at the library

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

From story time to job applications, libraries help whole communities. Library technicians keep them running.

This job mixes organizing, computers, and customer service. Explore what it feels like before deciding whether it interests you.

Go at your pace. The short questions at the end are for reflection. There are no right or wrong answers.

2What is a Library Technician?

You help librarians run the library. You check materials in and out, help visitors, and keep everything organized.

  • Check books and other materials in and out.
  • Answer simple questions and help people find things.
  • Help visitors use computers, printers, and online resources.
  • Process new books and materials.
  • Sort and shelve materials.
  • Help with programs and events.

The real picture: Titles vary, such as library assistant, library associate, or circulation clerk.

3A Day in the Life

One possible day: Every library is different.

  • Morning: Check in returned books and sort them.
  • Later: Help visitors at the front desk.
  • Midday: Help someone print a job application.
  • Afternoon: Shelve books and process new arrivals.
  • Before leaving: Tidy the shelves and set up for an evening program.

Some hours are quiet. Others are busy with families and students.

4How You Learn the Job

Paths vary. Some libraries hire people with a high school diploma; others want an associate degree, a certificate, or a bachelor’s degree.

  • On-the-job training: Libraries teach their computer system and procedures.
  • Online programs: Library technician certificates and associate degrees are offered online by colleges in other states.
  • Start nearby: Library page or volunteer roles can open the door.

Paying for training:

  1. Start with financial aid, scholarships, and grants. The school's financial aid office can help.
  2. Ask employers whether they help pay for training.
  3. Ask for the full cost, including books, fees, travel, and a computer.
  4. Ask your career counselor about other help, such as WIOA. WIOA is a federal program that can help some people pay for job training. Ask before you enroll.

Employer help with school may come with a promise to keep working there. Read the terms first.

5Training and Employer Requirements

We did not find a North Dakota license for library technicians. Requirements depend on the employer. Ask what you need.

  • Comfort with computers and careful organizing.
  • Friendly, patient customer service.
  • A background check for school libraries.
  • Ability to push carts and reach shelves, or ask about the physical demands.

Before you begin: Ask how new staff are trained.

Ask about written instructions, an accessible workstation, extra practice time, or other support that helps you learn and do the work well.

6Pay and Job Outlook

North Dakota library technician pay — May 2025:

  • Middle pay: $18.87 an hour ($39,240 a year). Half of workers earned more and half earned less.
  • Half of workers earned between $17.45 and $21.87 an hour.
  • 1 in 10 earned less than $16.78 an hour, and 1 in 10 earned more than $24.08.
  • These numbers show what workers earned. They are not a promise of what a new worker will start at.
  • North Dakota had about 400 of these jobs in 2024. The state projects about 410 in 2034, a change of +1%. That works out to about 70 openings a year, mostly from people who retire or change jobs.

Where people work: Public libraries, school libraries, colleges and universities, and the State Library.

Work setting: Library front desks, shelves, and workrooms. Some jobs include evenings and Saturdays.

Compare an offer: Ask about hourly pay, hours per week, benefits, and training.

7Where This Can Lead

  • Senior technician: Handle cataloging or interlibrary loans.
  • Branch or department supervisor: Lead staff.
  • Librarian: With a master’s degree in library science.
  • School library aide: Work with students.

Ask library staff which paths are open.

8The Real Talk

Things you might like:

  • A calm, organized place to work.
  • Helping people learn.
  • Being around books and information.
  • Steady public jobs.

Things to ask about:

  • Modest pay.
  • Part-time jobs and evening or weekend hours.
  • Few openings.
  • Visitors who need a lot of patience.

North Dakota projects the number of these jobs to stay about the same from 2024 to 2034, with about 70 openings a year.

A question for yourself: Do you enjoy organizing things and helping people find what they need?
