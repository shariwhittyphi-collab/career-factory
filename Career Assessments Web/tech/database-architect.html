Database Architect Career Assessment

Designing data systems — ND in-demand occupation

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Database architects design how an organization stores and organizes its data.

This is an advanced role that usually comes after years of database work. You will explore whether it fits your goals.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Database Architect?

A database architect (also called data architect or data engineer) designs databases, data warehouses, and data pipelines. They decide how data is structured, stored, secured, and shared.

The Reality: Your designs shape how the whole organization uses data for years. It takes deep experience and constant learning.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Design database and data warehouse structures
  • Plan how data flows between systems
  • Set data standards
  • Plan data security
  • Choose database technology
  • Work with analysts and developers
  • Plan for growth
  • Write design documents

You design the foundation that data is built on.

3A Day in the Life

8:30 AM — Review a data model for a new reporting system.

10:00 AM — Meet analysts about what data they need.

11:30 AM — Design a data pipeline.

12:30 PM — Lunch.

1:30 PM — Evaluate a cloud data warehouse.

3:00 PM — Review security with the security team.

4:00 PM — Document the design.

Big-picture design work with data.

4Training in North Dakota

Usually a bachelor's degree plus several years of experience. Most architects start as DBAs, developers, or data analysts.

Training Pathways:

  • Bachelor's degree: CS, CIS, or data science.
  • Experience: DBA, developer, or data analyst roles first.
  • Cloud data certifications.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

  • University of North Dakota (Grand Forks or online) — B.S. Computer Science; BBA Information Systems
  • North Dakota State University (Fargo) — B.S. Computer Science (tracks incl. Data Science, Software Engineering, Cybersecurity); Information Technology major
  • Minot State University (Minot or online) — B.S. Computer Information Systems; B.S. Computer Science
  • Valley City State University (Valley City or online) — B.S. Computer Information Systems; Software Engineering
  • Dickinson State University (Dickinson, Bismarck, or online) — B.S. Computer Science; B.S. Computer Technology Management
  • University of Mary (Bismarck) — B.S. Computer Information Systems; B.S. Computer Science
  • UND (online or on campus) and NDSU — Data Science degrees
  • Minot State (online or on campus) — B.S. Data Science

Before You Start: Bachelor's degree and several years of data experience.

Tip: Start with financial aid, scholarships, and any help an employer offers — that is where the money starts. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like training, exam fees, or a laptop. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you sign up rather than after. Many IT certifications are also sold as low-cost online courses you can do at home.

5Certification & Licensing

No license. Cloud data certifications help. Employers value cloud data credentials.

Certifications Employers Look For:

  • AWS Certified Data Engineer – Associate
  • Microsoft Azure data certifications

The Exams: Computer-based.

Cost: AWS Data Engineer Associate $150. Microsoft prices vary by country.

Keeping It Current: Every 1–3 years.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Database Architect Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $41.52–$48.98/hour ($86,370–$101,880/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $52.61/hour ($109,430/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $62.34–$66.47/hour ($129,660–$138,250/year)

What that means: Very high pay, even at the lower end. Most workers have years of experience.

Jobs in North Dakota: North Dakota does not publish job projections for this career because it is so small. Nationally, jobs are expected to grow about 9% from 2024 to 2034 (much faster than average), with about 4,000 openings a year.

Outlook: Strong nationally, but few jobs in ND. Many roles are remote.

Who Hires in ND: State government (NDIT in Bismarck), Microsoft's Fargo campus, banks and insurance companies, health systems, energy companies, universities, and IT service companies. Many remote jobs exist.

Work Setting: Offices or remote. Regular hours.

Physical Demands: Light. Desk work.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 15-1243); U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034 (ND projections not published), via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Enterprise Architect — Plan all technology.

Path 2: Chief Data Officer — Lead data strategy.

Path 3: Data Engineering Manager — Lead a team.

Path 4: Consultant — Design for many clients.

8The Real Talk

What's Hard:

  • Few ND jobs
