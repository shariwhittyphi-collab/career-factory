Light Truck Driver Career Assessment

The highest median pay in this area

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Light truck drivers deliver goods in vans and smaller trucks — the ones that do not need a commercial licence.

The North Dakota median is about $23.05 an hour, the highest in this area, with about 12% growth expected by 2034. You need a driving licence and a clean record, not a degree.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Light Truck Driver?

You load your vehicle, run a route, deliver and sometimes collect, get signatures or scans, and handle the paperwork or the handheld that goes with it. Between stops you are driving; at stops you are lifting, wheeling, and talking to people. Most drivers plan at least part of their own route and sequence, which is more judgement than the job gets credit for.

The Reality: If nobody drives, nothing arrives. The store does not get stock, the clinic does not get supplies, the part does not reach the job site. On the job itself: you are alone most of the day, which suits some people and does not suit others. The pressure is the clock — routes are built tight, and traffic, weather, and a customer who is not there all come out of your time rather than the schedule’s. North Dakota winter driving is a real part of this job, not a footnote. And it is more physical than people think, because every delivery is a lift.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Load and secure the vehicle in delivery order
  • Run a route and manage the sequence and timing
  • Deliver, collect signatures or scans, and handle returns
  • Lift and wheel goods into buildings
  • Keep delivery records and paperwork accurate
  • Do pre-trip and post-trip vehicle checks
  • Report vehicle faults and damage
  • Drive safely in winter conditions
  • Deal with customers at the door, including unhappy ones

Running a route well is a planning job as much as a driving job.

3A Day in the Life

6:00 AM — Clock in. Pre-trip check on the van.

6:20 AM — Load in delivery order — last stop goes in first.

7:00 AM — First stops. Commercial deliveries before businesses get busy.

9:30 AM — Residential stretch. Several lifts, one with stairs.

11:30 AM — Lunch in the truck or at a stop.

12:00 PM — Afternoon stops. Re-sequence around a closed road.

2:30 PM — A customer is not there — attempt, log it, carry it forward.

4:00 PM — Back to the depot. Returns unloaded, paperwork in, post-trip check.

Mostly on your own, with the clock and the weather as the two pressures.

4Training in North Dakota

No school needed. You need a driving licence and a clean record. Employers train you on the route, the vehicle, the handheld, and the paperwork. Most will put you with an experienced driver for your first week or two.

Training Pathways:

  • Direct hire: Parcel companies, food and beverage distributors, parts suppliers, building suppliers, pharmacies, and florists all hire light truck drivers across North Dakota.
  • From warehouse work: One of the most common routes in. If you are already on a dock, ask about driving — median is about $1.10 an hour higher and far easier on your back.
  • Toward a CDL: This is the serious step up. A commercial licence opens heavy truck driving, which pays substantially more, and a number of North Dakota employers will pay for the training if you commit to staying. Ask at every interview whether they do.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

Before You Start: You need a valid driving licence and a driving record the employer’s insurer will accept — that is the real gate on this job, more than anything else. Most employers require you to be 21 for insurance reasons, and some positions require a DOT medical card. Ask what the record requirement actually is rather than assuming.

Tip: Most of these jobs need no school, so the cost of starting is not tuition — it is the things you need in the first month. Non-slip shoes, work clothes or a uniform, a way to get to work, and getting through the two or three weeks before the first paycheck arrives. Ask the employer first: many will supply a uniform, some give a shoe allowance, and some pay weekly rather than every two weeks, which matters a lot at the start. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like work clothes, boots, tools, or getting to work. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you start rather than after.

5Certification & Licensing

A driving licence is the requirement. No special certificate for most positions. Your licence and your record are what get you hired. Some vehicles and some loads add requirements, and employers normally arrange those.

What may be required:

  • Valid driving licence and an acceptable record — the actual gate on this job
  • DOT medical examiner’s certificate — required for some vehicles and routes; a physical, usually employer-paid
  • Hazmat or specific load endorsements — only for certain cargo, and employer-arranged
  • Commercial Driver’s Licence (CDL) — not needed for this job, but it is the main step up, and some employers pay for it
  • Forklift certification — useful if you also load, and employer-provided

The Exams: A DOT physical is an examination, not a test you study for. A CDL involves written tests and a skills test.

Cost: Nothing beyond a licence you already have. DOT physicals are usually employer-paid — ask. Never pay for CDL training yourself before asking whether an employer will cover it.

Keeping It Current: DOT medical cards are typically valid up to two years. A CDL renews with your licence.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Light Truck Driver Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $16.23–$18.24/hour ($33,750–$37,950/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $23.05/hour ($47,950/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $27.00–$44.44/hour ($56,160–$92,440/year)

What that means: Read this range carefully. The jump from the seventy-fifth percentile ($27.00) to the ninetieth ($44.44) is very steep, and that top figure reflects a small number of specialized and energy-sector driving positions rather than ordinary delivery work. Do not plan around it. A realistic expectation for a new light truck driver in North Dakota is somewhere between $16 and $23 an hour, rising with experience and a clean record. Overtime is common and can add a lot.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 2,360 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 2,630 (about 12% growth). Expect about 280 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is growing faster than average.

Outlook: Strong growth of about 12% in North Dakota by 2034, with roughly 280 openings a year. Online ordering and home delivery keep pushing this upward, and the state’s distances mean somebody has to drive them.

Who Hires in ND: Parcel and courier companies, food and beverage distributors, auto and farm parts suppliers, building and plumbing suppliers, pharmacies, laundry and uniform services, and florists.

Work Setting: In a vehicle most of the day, alone. In and out in all weather. Early starts are normal. Winter driving on rural North Dakota roads is part of the job.

Physical Demands: Moderate to high. Repeated lifting at every stop, climbing in and out of the vehicle dozens of times a day, stairs, and long periods seated between.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 53-3033); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Light Truck Driver — Where you start.

Path 2: Route Driver or Driver-Salesperson — Your own established route, often with a commission element.

Path 3: Lead Driver or Dispatcher — Routing and coordinating other drivers.

Path 4: Heavy Truck Driver (CDL) — The big step up in pay. See the transportation area of this site.

Path 5: Delivery Supervisor — Running a depot and a driver crew.

Path 6: Owner-Operator — Running your own vehicle. Higher pay, and real business risk.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • The highest median pay in this area at about $23.05 an hour
  • About 12% growth and steady openings
  • No school or degree needed
  • You work on your own most of the day
  • Easier on your back than dock or warehouse work
  • A clear, well-paid route onward through a CDL, which employers sometimes fund
  • Overtime is common and can add substantially

What's Hard:

  • Your driving record is the gate, and a poor one can keep you out
  • Winter driving in North Dakota, which is a genuine hazard and not a formality
  • Routes are built tight, and traffic and weather come out of your time
  • A lift at every stop, and stairs when there are stairs
  • Early starts
  • Alone most of the day, which does not suit everyone
  • Many employers require you to be 21 for insurance

Be Honest: Is your driving record clean enough for an insurer? Are you genuinely all right driving North Dakota roads in January? And are you comfortable being on your own for most of a working day?
