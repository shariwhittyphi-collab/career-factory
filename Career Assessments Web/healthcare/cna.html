Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA)

Career Assessment Tool — North Dakota

This tool will help you decide if becoming a Certified Nursing Assistant is the right choice for you.

You will learn about what CNAs really do, how to get trained in North Dakota, and what comes next in your career.

Important: Answer all questions honestly. This is just for you—there are no wrong answers.

What is a Certified Nursing Assistant?

A Certified Nursing Assistant, or CNA, helps sick and injured people in hospitals, nursing homes, and clinics. CNAs work with nurses and doctors to take care of patients. They do hands-on work like helping patients wash, eat, and use the bathroom. This is an entry-level job in healthcare. You work under the supervision of a Registered Nurse (RN) or Licensed Practical Nurse (LPN).

What Will You Actually Be Doing Every Day?

Here are the real tasks CNAs do:

  • Help patients take baths and get dressed
  • Assist patients with eating meals
  • Help patients use the toilet and change bedpans
  • Take vital signs (blood pressure, temperature, pulse, breathing)
  • Record vital signs and patient information on charts
  • Help patients move and change positions (lifting)
  • Change bed sheets and keep rooms clean
  • Watch patients for changes in their condition
  • Report changes to the nurse right away
  • Talk to patients and listen to their concerns
  • Work with the nursing team to help patients feel better
What You Need to Know: This is hard work. You will be on your feet most of the time. You will lift and move patients who are heavy. You will help with toileting and deal with bodily fluids. Some patients are confused or angry. You might work nights, weekends, and holidays. The work is physical and emotional.

What Training Do You Need in North Dakota?

The Requirements:

  • Take an approved Nurse Aide Training Program (NATP)
  • Complete 75 hours of training:
    • Classroom learning (online or in person)
    • Hands-on practice in a skills lab and with real patients
    • (Important: 16 hours of training on safety, infection control, communication, and patient rights must happen BEFORE you work with patients)
  • Pass the North Dakota Headmaster exam (the state test)
  • Register with North Dakota Nurse Aide Registry

Timeline and Cost:

Item Details
How long does training take? About 4 to 7 weeks at most colleges (Lake Region: 4 weeks; Bismarck State: 5 to 7 weeks)
How much does it cost? About $850 to $900 at Lake Region and Bismarck State (2026, textbook included). The state exam costs extra. Costs vary, so check with the program.
Do employers help pay? Yes! If a nursing home hires you (or offers you a job) before you start training, it cannot charge you for training

Where Can You Get Training in North Dakota?

  • Bismarck State College (Bismarck; in-person or hybrid)
  • Lake Region State College (Devils Lake; online classes with in-person lab and clinical)
  • North Dakota State College of Science (NDSCS)
  • Williston State College (online)
  • Dickinson State University (Dickinson)
  • Career and technical centers, and some nursing homes and hospitals. Ask ND Health and Human Services for the full list of approved programs.

What is the Headmaster Exam?

After you finish your 75 hours of training, you take the Headmaster exam. This is the North Dakota state test for CNAs. Here is what it tests:

Written Knowledge Test:

This part tests what you learned in the classroom:

  • How to keep patients safe and prevent falls
  • How to stop germs from spreading (infection control)
  • How to talk to and help patients
  • The body and how organs work
  • Laws and rules for CNAs
  • How to keep yourself healthy and safe at work
  • When to report problems to the nurse

Skills Test (Hands-On):

You show that you can actually do CNA tasks. The test includes things like:

  • Take someone's blood pressure correctly
  • Help a patient wash their hands
  • Help a patient use the toilet safely
  • Move and turn a patient in bed without hurting them
  • Talk to a patient who is confused
  • Keep a patient's room clean and safe
  • Follow all safety rules
About the Exam: You get three tries to pass both parts (written and skills). If you do not pass in three tries, you must take an approved training program again before you can test again.

What Comes After CNA?

Being a CNA can lead to many different paths:

Path 1: Become a Licensed Practical Nurse (LPN)

This takes about 1 more year (3 semesters in the Dakota Nursing Program). You learn more advanced nursing skills. LPNs make more money than CNAs.

Path 2: Become a Registered Nurse (RN)

This takes 2 to 4 more years. You earn an Associate degree or Bachelor degree in nursing. RNs have more responsibility and make more money.

Path 3: Specialize in One Area

You can get extra training to specialize, such as:

  • Wound care (caring for healing injuries)
  • Dementia or Alzheimer care (caring for people with memory loss)
  • Hospice care (caring for people at the end of life)
  • Pediatric care (caring for children)
  • Dialysis assistance

Path 4: Stay as a CNA and Advance

Many people work as CNAs their whole career and are very happy. You can become a senior CNA, train new CNAs, or supervise other CNAs. Direct patient care is important work.

Should You Become a CNA?

CNAs are important. You do real work that helps real people.

CNA is a good choice if:

  • You want to help people and make them feel better
  • You can handle hard, physical work
  • You do not mind working nights, weekends, and holidays
  • You want to start a healthcare career quickly
  • You want to earn money while you explore other nursing jobs

Think twice if:

  • You only want this job because you need money fast
  • You cannot handle being around sick or dying people
  • You cannot do heavy lifting or physical work
  • You do not want to work nights and weekends
  • You are not interested in healthcare at all
