Recreation Worker Career Assessment

Leading activities, games, and programs that help people have fun and stay active

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Summer programs, community events, and senior center classes all need someone to plan the fun and keep it safe.

This job mixes activity, leadership, and people skills. Explore what it feels like before deciding whether it interests you.

Go at your pace. The short questions at the end are for reflection. There are no right or wrong answers.

2What is a Recreation Worker?

You plan and lead activities like sports, games, crafts, and fitness classes in parks, camps, and community centers.

  • Set up equipment and spaces.
  • Lead games, sports, crafts, and classes.
  • Plan activities that fit each group.
  • Enforce safety rules.
  • Give first aid when needed.
  • Keep attendance and supply records.

The real picture: Many jobs are part time or seasonal. Full-time jobs often involve more planning and leadership.

3A Day in the Life

One possible day: Every program is different.

  • Morning: Set up the field for a youth soccer program.
  • Later: Lead drills and games.
  • Midday: Lead a chair exercise class at the senior center.
  • Afternoon: Plan next week’s activities.
  • Before leaving: Put away equipment and write down attendance.

Weather and turnout can change the plan.

4How You Learn the Job

Many part-time jobs need only a high school diploma, and some take teens. Full-time jobs often want a college degree in recreation or a related field.

  • On-the-job training: Employers teach their programs and safety rules.
  • Certifications: CPR and first aid are common. Pool jobs need lifeguard certification.
  • Start nearby: Volunteering or summer camp jobs build experience.

Paying for training:

  1. Start with financial aid, scholarships, and grants. The school's financial aid office can help.
  2. Ask employers whether they help pay for training.
  3. Ask for the full cost, including books, fees, travel, and a computer.
  4. Ask your career counselor about other help, such as WIOA. WIOA is a federal program that can help some people pay for job training. Ask before you enroll.

Employer help with school may come with a promise to keep working there. Read the terms first.

5Training and Employer Requirements

We did not find a North Dakota license for recreation workers. Requirements depend on the employer. Ask what you need.

  • CPR and first aid certification.
  • A background check for work with children or vulnerable adults.
  • Ability to be active and on your feet.
  • Willingness to work evenings, weekends, or summers.

Before you begin: Ask what training is provided and how staff handle emergencies.

Ask about written instructions, an accessible workstation, extra practice time, or other support that helps you learn and do the work well.

6Pay and Job Outlook

North Dakota recreation worker pay — May 2025:

  • Middle pay: $17.94 an hour ($37,310 a year). Half of workers earned more and half earned less.
  • Half of workers earned between $14.61 and $22.69 an hour.
  • 1 in 10 earned less than $10.70 an hour, and 1 in 10 earned more than $25.96.
  • These numbers show what workers earned. They are not a promise of what a new worker will start at.
  • North Dakota had about 960 of these jobs in 2024. The state projects about 1,040 in 2034, a change of +8%. That works out to about 210 openings a year, mostly from people who retire or change jobs.

Where people work: Park districts, cities, YMCAs, camps, senior centers, nursing homes, and after-school programs.

Work setting: Parks, gyms, camps, and community centers. Many jobs are part time or seasonal.

Compare an offer: Ask about hourly pay, hours per week, season length, and whether full-time work is available.

7Where This Can Lead

  • Program leader: Plan and run whole programs.
  • Recreation coordinator: Lead staff and budgets.
  • Recreation therapy: With a degree, help people heal through activities.
  • Parks and recreation manager: Run a park district.

Ask your supervisor which paths are open.

8The Real Talk

Things you might like:

  • Fun, active work.
  • Making a difference for kids or older adults.
  • Starting part time without a degree.
  • Summer jobs outdoors.

Things to ask about:

  • Lower pay in many part-time jobs.
  • Seasonal work.
  • Evenings and weekends.
  • Managing behavior in large groups.

North Dakota projects about 8% growth in these jobs from 2024 to 2034, with about 210 openings a year.

A question for yourself: Do you enjoy leading activities and helping others have a good time?
