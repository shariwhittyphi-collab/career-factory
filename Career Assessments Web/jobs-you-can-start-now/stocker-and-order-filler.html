Stocker and Order Filler Career Assessment

Steady growth and about 870 openings a year

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Stockers and order fillers move product from the back of a store or warehouse onto shelves and into orders.

North Dakota expects about 12% growth by 2034 with roughly 870 openings a year. It pays about $3.20 an hour more than fast food and needs no school.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Stocker and Order Filler?

You unload deliveries, break down pallets, check what came against what was ordered, stock shelves in the right place with the right labels, rotate stock so the oldest sells first, and pull items to fill customer or store orders. In a warehouse it is more picking and packing; in a store it is more shelving and facing.

The Reality: If nobody stocks, the shelves empty and the store has nothing to sell — that is the whole business, stopped. The work itself is physical and repetitive, and a lot of it happens overnight or very early, because stores would rather you were not in the aisles when customers are. Night stocking pays a bit more and suits some people completely; it wrecks others. You will lift all shift, and you will do the same motion thousands of times. Count accuracy matters more than speed in most places, though you will be pushed on both.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Unload trucks and break down pallets
  • Check deliveries against the order and report shortages
  • Stock shelves in the right place with the right price label
  • Rotate stock so the oldest goes out first
  • Pull and pack items to fill orders
  • Keep the backroom organized so things can be found
  • Operate a pallet jack, and sometimes a forklift
  • Flatten boxes and keep the aisles clear and safe
  • Help customers who stop you in the aisle

A well-stocked store looks like nothing happened, which is exactly the point.

3A Day in the Life

10:00 PM — Clock in for the night shift. See what is on the truck.

10:30 PM — Unload and sort pallets by department.

11:30 PM — Start stocking. Dairy and frozen first — they cannot wait.

1:00 AM — Break.

1:30 AM — Dry goods. Rotate dates as you go.

3:30 AM — Face the shelves and clear the aisles.

5:00 AM — Break down boxes, tidy the backroom.

6:00 AM — Hand over to the day crew.

Overnight shifts are common. Day and evening stocking exists too, especially in warehouses.

4Training in North Dakota

No school needed. Trained on the job in a few days. You learn the layout, the scanner, and the rotation rules by doing them. Forklift certification, if the job needs it, is almost always provided and paid for by the employer.

Training Pathways:

  • Retail and grocery stocking: Hired year-round, often for overnight shifts. The quickest way in.
  • Warehouse order filling: Usually better paid than store stocking, and often day shifts. Distribution centers in Fargo, Bismarck, Grand Forks, and Minot hire steadily.
  • Forklift certification: A short employer-provided course. Once you have it, it follows you to the next job and is worth real money.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

Before You Start: No diploma or experience required. You need to lift repeatedly through a shift and work accurately. Some positions require you to be 18 for equipment. A clean record is often asked for in distribution, but ask rather than assume.

Tip: Most of these jobs need no school, so the cost of starting is not tuition — it is the things you need in the first month. Non-slip shoes, work clothes or a uniform, a way to get to work, and getting through the two or three weeks before the first paycheck arrives. Ask the employer first: many will supply a uniform, some give a shoe allowance, and some pay weekly rather than every two weeks, which matters a lot at the start. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like work clothes, boots, tools, or getting to work. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you start rather than after.

5Certification & Licensing

No license required. Forklift certification is worth getting. You can start with nothing. Forklift certification is the one credential in this job that measurably raises what you can earn, and employers normally provide it free.

Worth having:

  • Forklift (powered industrial truck) certification — required by law to operate one, employer-provided and employer-paid in almost all cases
  • Pallet jack and order picker training — provided on the job
  • OSHA 10 — a general safety course some warehouse employers like to see
  • Food safety basics — relevant if you stock grocery or cold storage

The Exams: Forklift certification involves a short written test and a hands-on driving evaluation.

Cost: Normally nothing — employers pay for it because the law requires them to train you.

Keeping It Current: Forklift certification must be renewed every three years.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Stocker and Order Filler Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $15.16–$17.05/hour ($31,530–$35,470/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $17.69/hour ($36,790/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $21.10–$22.95/hour ($43,890–$47,740/year)

What that means: Warehouses and distribution centers generally pay above the median here; grocery and retail stocking sits nearer the bottom. Overnight shifts usually carry a shift premium — ask what it is. Forklift certification moves you up this range faster than anything else.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 4,920 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 5,500 (about 12% growth). Expect about 870 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is growing faster than average.

Outlook: Strong. About 12% growth in North Dakota by 2034 and roughly 870 openings a year. Online ordering keeps pushing warehouse and distribution work upward.

Who Hires in ND: Grocery stores, big-box retailers, hardware and farm supply stores, distribution centers, food distributors, pharmacies, and parts warehouses.

Work Setting: Indoors, often overnight or early morning in retail, more often day shifts in warehouses. Cold storage work means working in the cold all shift.

Physical Demands: High. Repeated lifting, usually up to 50 pounds, bending, reaching, and walking for the whole shift. Hard on the lower back over years.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 53-7065); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Stocker or Order Filler — Where you start.

Path 2: Forklift Operator — Certification is free and usually moves you up the pay range.

Path 3: Receiving Clerk — Checking in deliveries and handling paperwork.

Path 4: Inventory Control — Tracking stock and investigating discrepancies.

Path 5: Warehouse Lead or Supervisor — Running a crew and a shift.

Path 6: Freight and Material Mover — Median about $4.30 an hour higher — see that page.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • About 12% growth and 870 openings a year
  • Pays about $3.20 an hour more than fast food
  • No school or experience needed
  • Free forklift certification that follows you anywhere
  • Mostly working on your own or with a small crew, not with the public
  • Clear route into warehouse and logistics work that pays better

What's Hard:

  • Overnight and early-morning shifts are common in retail
  • Repeated lifting all shift, hard on the back over years
  • Repetitive and sometimes monotonous
  • Production targets in warehouses can be tight
  • Cold storage work means being cold for eight hours
  • Night shifts are hard on sleep and on family life

Be Honest: Can your back take lifting all shift for years, not months? Can you work overnight without it wrecking the rest of your life? Can you stay accurate when you are being pushed to go faster?
