Heavy Equipment Mechanic Career Assessment

Repairing bulldozers, loaders, and construction equipment

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Heavy equipment mechanics repair the bulldozers, excavators, loaders, and cranes used in construction, mining, and energy.

You will explore what this big-machine trade is really like and whether it fits you.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Heavy Equipment Mechanic?

A heavy equipment mechanic inspects and repairs large mobile machines. They work on hydraulics, transmissions, brakes, electrical systems, and undercarriages. Many work for Caterpillar or Komatsu dealers or construction and mining companies.

The Reality: These machines are huge and parts are very heavy. Many repairs happen on job sites and mines, outdoors, in all weather.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Diagnose problems with hydraulics, engines, and electronics
  • Repair and replace large parts
  • Service undercarriages and tracks
  • Do preventive maintenance
  • Make field repairs at job sites
  • Use cranes and hoists to move parts
  • Test equipment after repairs
  • Keep service records

You keep the biggest machines in North Dakota working.

3A Day in the Life

6:00 AM — Drive the service truck to a road construction site.

7:00 AM — An excavator has a hydraulic leak. You find a cracked hose and replace it.

10:00 AM — Preventive maintenance on a loader: fluids, filters, and inspection.

12:00 PM — Lunch in the truck.

1:00 PM — Back to the dealer shop. Rebuild a transmission with a teammate.

4:00 PM — Diagnose an electrical fault with the laptop.

5:30 PM — Write up your work.

Big machines, big parts, and a mix of shop and field work.

4Training in North Dakota

No state license is required. Dealer-sponsored programs and diesel programs are common paths. Registered apprenticeships are also available in ND.

Training Pathways:

  • Dealer-sponsored AAS (about 2 years): Caterpillar or Komatsu programs with paid internships.
  • Diesel technology program: Leads to heavy equipment jobs too.
  • Registered apprenticeship: ApprenticeshipND lists Construction Equipment Mechanics.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

  • ND State College of Science (Wahpeton) — Caterpillar Dealer Service Technician, AAS
  • ND State College of Science (Wahpeton) — Diesel Technology – Komatsu, AAS
  • Williston State College (Williston) — Diesel Technology, AAS or certificate
  • Bismarck State College (Bismarck) — Diesel Technology certificate (heavy equipment technician is a listed career)

Before You Start: High school diploma or GED. A valid driver's license. A CDL may be required later. You will likely need your own tools.

Tip: Start with financial aid, scholarships, and any help an employer offers — that is where the money starts. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like training, tools, work boots, or exam fees. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you sign up rather than after. Registered apprenticeships let you earn a paycheck while you learn — see jobsnd.com/job-seeker/apprenticeships for ND programs.

5Certification & Licensing

No license needed. Dealer and manufacturer training matters most.

Certifications Employers Look For:

  • Caterpillar or Komatsu dealer training
  • ASE truck tests — helpful for diesel skills
  • CDL — often required to drive service trucks

The Exams: Dealer training is completed through the program and employer.

Cost: Dealer-sponsored programs may help with costs. Ask the dealer and school.

Keeping It Current: Keep up with manufacturer training.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Mobile Heavy Equipment Mechanic Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $25.00–$29.31/hour ($52,000–$60,960/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $36.55/hour ($76,030/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $42.19–$48.96/hour ($87,750–$101,830/year)

What that means: ND pay is well above the national median. Field and mining jobs often include overtime.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 2,120 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 2,340 (about 10% growth). Expect about 200 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is about 6% growth — faster than average.

Outlook: Very good, with about 200 openings a year in ND. Construction, mining, and energy projects keep demand strong.

Who Hires in ND: Caterpillar and Komatsu dealerships, construction companies, coal mines, oil field service companies, and state and county road departments.

Work Setting: Shops, construction sites, and mines. Field work in all weather. Some jobs involve travel.

Physical Demands: High. Very heavy parts, climbing on machines, working outdoors in cold, heat, mud, and dust.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 49-3042); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Field Service Technician — Travel to job sites.

Path 2: Master Technician — Top-level dealer tech.

Path 3: Hydraulics Specialist — Focus on hydraulic systems.

Path 4: Shop Foreman — Lead the shop.

Path 5: Equipment Manager — Manage a company's fleet.

Path 6: Trainer — Teach new techs.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • High pay in ND
  • Lots of openings
  • Big, powerful machines
  • Paid internships in dealer programs
  • Overtime opportunities
  • Growing demand

What's Hard:

  • Very physical
  • Outdoor work in all weather
  • Heavy parts and safety risks
  • Buying tools is expensive
  • Travel to job sites
  • Long days in busy seasons

Be Honest: Can you handle heavy, physical work outside? Are you OK with travel and long days? Do you like big machines and figuring out hydraulic problems?
