Fast Food and Counter Worker Career Assessment

The most openings of any job in North Dakota

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Fast food and counter workers take orders, make food, and keep a restaurant running through a rush.

North Dakota has about 2,850 openings a year in this job — more than any other job on this whole site. You can usually start within a week or two of applying.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Fast Food and Counter Worker?

You take orders at a counter or drive-through, put food together to a set recipe, keep the line stocked, and clean as you go. Most places will train you in a few days. The skill is not any one task. It is tracking several orders at once, keeping everything organized and correct while the line builds, and staying pleasant with every single person — all at the same time. That is three jobs running in your head at once, and people who have never done it think it is one.

The Reality: This is the fastest-paced work on this site. You are on your feet the whole shift, often eight hours with two short breaks, and during a rush there is no pause. The pay starts low. And there is a part of this job nobody counts: you serve people with a smile all shift knowing most of them never really see you. Being pleasant on demand for eight hours is work. It is why people come home from a counter job worn out in a way that does not match what they physically did. People will treat this job as though it is not real work, and it is.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Take orders at the counter or drive-through
  • Make food to a set recipe, the same way every time
  • Keep the line stocked before you run out
  • Handle cash and cards accurately
  • Clean as you go and at close
  • Follow food safety and temperature rules
  • Work the timing so a whole order comes up together
  • Stay pleasant with every customer, including the ones who are not
  • Cover for whoever is behind

Done well, this is fast, coordinated, skilled work — and the fact that it looks easy from the other side of the counter is the proof of it.

3A Day in the Life

10:45 AM — Clock in before the lunch rush. Check what is low and stock it.

11:15 AM — Rush starts. Take orders and run the register.

12:00 PM — Peak. Orders stacking up. You are tracking four at once.

1:30 PM — Rush breaks. Restock, wipe down, catch your breath.

2:00 PM — Break. Sit down for the first time in three hours.

2:30 PM — Slower stretch. Prep for the dinner shift.

4:30 PM — Dinner rush begins. Same again.

7:00 PM — Clean the line, take out trash, hand over to the closers.

Two bursts of intensity with quieter stretches between.

4Training in North Dakota

No school needed. Most people are trained on the job in under a week. You do not need a diploma, experience, or a certificate to get hired. Training happens on shift, usually alongside someone more experienced for the first few days.

Training Pathways:

  • Walk-in or online application: Most places hire continuously. You can often be working within one to two weeks.
  • Food handler training: Some employers ask for it, and most pay for it or provide it. It is a short online course, not a school program.
  • Shift lead and management: Almost every fast food manager started on the line. Employers promote from inside because they need people who already know the store.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

Before You Start: No diploma or experience required at most employers. Must be able to stand for a full shift and work at speed. Some positions require you to be 16 or older; some shifts and equipment require 18.

Tip: Most of these jobs need no school, so the cost of starting is not tuition — it is the things you need in the first month. Non-slip shoes, work clothes or a uniform, a way to get to work, and getting through the two or three weeks before the first paycheck arrives. Ask the employer first: many will supply a uniform, some give a shoe allowance, and some pay weekly rather than every two weeks, which matters a lot at the start. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like work clothes, boots, tools, or getting to work. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you start rather than after.

5Certification & Licensing

No license or certificate is required. You do not need anything official to start. A few employers ask for a food handler card, and they usually arrange it.

Worth having anyway:

  • Food handler card — short online course, often paid for by the employer
  • ServSafe Food Handler — the more recognized version, useful if you want to move up or move to a restaurant kitchen
  • ServSafe Manager — needed for most shift-lead and manager roles; employers usually pay for it

The Exams: A food handler course ends in a short multiple-choice test you take online.

Cost: A food handler card is usually under $25 and is often paid by the employer. Ask before you pay for it yourself.

Keeping It Current: Food handler cards are typically good for two to three years.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Fast Food and Counter Worker Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $10.94–$13.80/hour ($22,750–$28,700/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $14.46/hour ($30,080/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $16.95–$17.97/hour ($35,250–$37,380/year)

What that means: The bottom of this range is part-time and brand-new. Shift leads sit near the top. Hours matter as much as the rate — many of these jobs are part-time, so 25 hours a week at $14.46 is not the same as full-time work. Ask how many hours you will actually get before you take the job.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 11,760 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 12,700 (about 8% growth). Expect about 2850 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is growing about as fast as average.

Outlook: The largest number of openings of any job on this site — about 2,850 a year in North Dakota. Most of that is people leaving and being replaced, which is exactly why it is easy to get hired.

Who Hires in ND: Fast food chains, coffee shops, convenience stores, gas station food counters, cafeterias, and food courts. Every town in North Dakota has these.

Work Setting: Indoors, hot near the line, loud during a rush. Shifts include early mornings, nights, weekends, and holidays. Schedules often change week to week.

Physical Demands: High. On your feet the whole shift, constant movement, lifting up to about 25 pounds, hot surfaces, wet floors.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 35-3023); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Crew Member — Where nearly everyone starts.

Path 2: Trainer — Train new hires. Usually a small raise.

Path 3: Shift Lead — Run the store for a shift. First real step up.

Path 4: Assistant Manager — Scheduling, ordering, handling problems.

Path 5: General Manager — Run the whole store. Salaried.

Path 6: Restaurant Cook or Prep — Take the speed and food safety into a kitchen, where the median is about $3 an hour higher.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • Hired fast, often within two weeks
  • No school, diploma, or experience needed
  • Available in every ND town
  • Free or cheap meals on shift
  • Promotion from inside is genuinely common
  • Builds speed and composure that transfer anywhere

What's Hard:

  • Pay starts low
  • Standing and moving for the whole shift
  • Schedules change week to week
  • Nights, weekends, and holidays
  • Being pleasant on demand all shift, to people who do not really see you
  • Hours are often part-time, so the weekly total can be small

Be Honest: Can you keep track of several things at once when it gets loud? Can you be pleasant with people for eight hours when most of them are not really looking at you? Can you handle a schedule that changes week to week?
