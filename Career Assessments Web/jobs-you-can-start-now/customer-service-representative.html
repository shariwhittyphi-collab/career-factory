Customer Service Representative Career Assessment

The best-paying desk job in this area

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Customer service representatives handle calls, emails, and chats from customers — answering questions, fixing problems, and taking orders or payments.

The North Dakota median is about $21.48 an hour, the highest of any desk job in this area, with roughly 810 openings a year. No degree is required at most employers.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Customer Service Representative?

You take contacts one after another — by phone, email, or chat — find out what the person actually needs, look it up in the company’s systems, and either solve it or get it to someone who can. You document everything as you go. Most of the skill is listening properly to someone who is upset and explaining something clearly to someone who does not know the vocabulary.

The Reality: If nobody does this job, every problem a company causes stays unsolved. That is why it exists, and it is also why the calls are rarely cheerful — people do not call when things are working. You absorb frustration all day for things you did not do, and then take the next call thirty seconds later with none of it in your voice. Most places measure you: calls handled, time per call, how fast you answer, customer ratings. That measurement can be reasonable or it can be brutal, and it varies enormously by employer. Ask about it directly before you accept a job, because it is the single biggest difference between a good customer service job and a miserable one.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Answer calls, emails, and chats from customers
  • Find out what the person actually needs, not just what they first said
  • Look up accounts, orders, and records in company systems
  • Explain policies, charges, and next steps clearly
  • Solve the problem or escalate it to the right person
  • Document every contact accurately
  • Take orders, payments, and account changes
  • Keep calm with people who are angry about something you did not cause
  • Meet handling and quality targets

Staying genuinely useful on the fortieth call of the day is the skill, and it is harder than any single call looks.

3A Day in the Life

8:00 AM — Log in, check overnight notes and any system issues.

8:15 AM — Calls start. First one is a billing question.

9:30 AM — A difficult call. Stay calm, get it resolved, document it.

10:15 AM — Break.

10:30 AM — Back on. Emails and chats between calls.

12:00 PM — Lunch.

12:30 PM — Afternoon volume. Steady.

2:30 PM — Short team meeting on a policy change.

4:30 PM — Finish notes, clear follow-ups, log out.

Indoors at a desk, contact after contact, with your day shaped by call volume.

4Training in North Dakota

No degree needed. Employers train you, usually over two to four weeks. This is one of the few jobs in this area with a real paid training period. You learn the products, the systems, and the policies in a classroom-style group before you take live contacts, then sit with a coach for your first weeks.

Training Pathways:

  • Direct hire with paid training: The standard route. Banks, insurers, utilities, health plans, and retailers in North Dakota hire in groups and train you on the clock.
  • Remote and hybrid positions: Genuinely common in this job now, which matters if you are rural or do not drive. Ask whether the employer offers it and whether they provide the equipment.
  • From retail or counter work: A very natural move, and worth roughly $4 an hour over a counter job. Say in your application that you have dealt with upset customers in person, because that is harder than doing it by phone.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

Before You Start: Most employers ask for a high school diploma or equivalent. You need clear speech, reasonable typing and computer skills, and the composure to stay level with someone who is angry. Many positions require a background check — ask what they look at rather than assuming the answer is no.

Tip: Most of these jobs need no school, so the cost of starting is not tuition — it is the things you need in the first month. Non-slip shoes, work clothes or a uniform, a way to get to work, and getting through the two or three weeks before the first paycheck arrives. Ask the employer first: many will supply a uniform, some give a shoe allowance, and some pay weekly rather than every two weeks, which matters a lot at the start. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like work clothes, boots, tools, or getting to work. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you start rather than after.

5Certification & Licensing

No license or certificate is required for most positions. Nothing is needed up front. Some industries require a license you get after you are hired, and the employer pays for it.

Industry-specific, and employer-paid:

  • Insurance producer license — required if you sell or service insurance; employers normally pay for the course and exam
  • Securities licenses (SIE, Series 6 or 7) — required in some financial services roles; employer-sponsored
  • HIPAA training — required in health insurance and provider roles; employer-provided
  • Call center or contact center certificates — optional, and rarely asked for by employers here

The Exams: Industry licenses involve a proctored exam, taken after you are hired and with paid study time at most employers.

Cost: Nothing for a general customer service position. Industry licenses are normally employer-paid — confirm that before you agree to anything.

Keeping It Current: Insurance and securities licenses require continuing education and periodic renewal, usually arranged by the employer.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Customer Service Representative Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $16.44–$18.55/hour ($34,190–$38,580/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $21.48/hour ($44,670/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $25.99–$32.86/hour ($54,060–$68,350/year)

What that means: Note how high the bottom of this range is: about $16.44 an hour to start, which is above the median of several jobs in this area. The top end is specialized work — insurance, financial services, and health plans — and team leads. Industry licensing is the clearest route up this range, and the employer usually pays for it.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 6,560 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 6,280 (a decline of about 4%). Expect about 810 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is declining.

Outlook: Declining in total positions — North Dakota expects about 4% fewer by 2034, from roughly 6,560 to 6,280 — but still about 810 openings a year because turnover is high. Self-service tools and automation are taking the simple contacts. What that leaves is the complicated ones, which is why the specialized end of this job is holding up better than the general end. If you go into this, aim at a licensed or specialized area rather than general inquiries.

Who Hires in ND: Banks and credit unions, insurance companies, health plans, utilities, telecom companies, retailers, healthcare systems, and government offices. Several large employers in Fargo, Bismarck, and Grand Forks hire in groups.

Work Setting: Indoors at a desk with a headset. Often open-plan and noisy. Remote and hybrid positions are genuinely common. Evening and weekend shifts exist, and many employers run business hours only.

Physical Demands: Low. Sitting for long periods, repeated typing, and headset use. The strain here is mental and vocal rather than physical.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 43-4051); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Customer Service Representative — Where you start, with paid training.

Path 2: Senior or Specialist Representative — Harder cases, better pay.

Path 3: Licensed Representative — Insurance or financial services. The clearest pay jump, and employer-paid.

Path 4: Team Lead or Supervisor — Coaching a team rather than taking contacts.

Path 5: Claims, Underwriting, or Account Management — Common moves out of the phones.

Path 6: Training or Quality — Teaching new hires or reviewing contacts.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • The best-paying desk job in this area at about $21.48 an hour
  • Paid training, which almost nothing else in this area offers
  • Remote and hybrid positions are genuinely available
  • Indoor, seated, year-round, weather-independent work
  • Employers pay for industry licenses that raise your pay substantially
  • Clear routes out of the phones into claims, underwriting, and account work

What's Hard:

  • Declining overall — about 4% fewer positions expected by 2034
  • You absorb other people’s anger about things you did not do
  • Measured closely on call times and ratings, and some employers are harsh about it
  • Back-to-back contacts with little recovery time
  • Scripts and policies you may not agree with and still have to deliver
  • Sitting all day, and the vocal and mental fatigue is real

Be Honest: Can you take the next call in a normal voice thirty seconds after a bad one? Can you work somewhere that measures your minutes? And will you ask at the interview exactly how you will be measured, because that is what decides whether this job is tolerable?
