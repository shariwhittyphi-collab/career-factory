Housekeeping Cleaner Career Assessment

Skilled, physical work on a clock

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Housekeeping cleaners make up rooms in hotels, hospitals, care homes, and offices.

It pays about $2.40 an hour more than fast food, needs no school, and about 550 positions open in North Dakota each year.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Housekeeping Cleaner?

You clean and reset rooms to a standard, on a set number of minutes per room. In a hotel that is stripping and remaking beds, bathrooms, floors, and restocking. In a hospital or care home it also means following infection-control rules that actually matter to whether patients get sick.

The Reality: The clock is the hard part. You are given a set time per room and a set number of rooms, and the day does not get longer when a room is worse than expected. It is heavy on your back, knees, and shoulders. Most people never see you, and the ones who do often look straight through you.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Strip and remake beds to a standard
  • Clean bathrooms, floors, and surfaces
  • Restock supplies and linens
  • Work through a room list in a set time per room
  • Use cleaning chemicals safely and in the right order
  • Follow infection-control rules in medical settings
  • Report damage and maintenance problems
  • Handle guests’ belongings honestly and carefully

A room that looks untouched is the whole job, and it is harder than it looks.

3A Day in the Life

8:00 AM — Clock in, get your room list and stock your cart.

8:20 AM — First room. Strip the bed, open the window, start the bathroom.

9:30 AM — Third room. One is a heavy checkout and runs long — make it up later.

11:00 AM — Restock the cart. Linen run.

12:00 PM — Lunch.

12:30 PM — Afternoon rooms. Report a broken shower head to maintenance.

2:30 PM — Go back to the room that ran long.

4:00 PM — Return the cart, log the rooms finished, hand over anything not done.

Steady physical work against a clock, mostly on your own.

4Training in North Dakota

No school needed. Trained on the job, usually within a week. You learn the routine and the order of tasks by working alongside someone experienced. Hospitals and care homes add infection-control training, which they provide.

Training Pathways:

  • Hotels: Hired year-round, more heavily before summer and during oil and construction activity. Trained on shift.
  • Hospitals and care homes (environmental services): Usually pay better than hotels and are steadier work. They train you in infection control and cleaning in patient areas.
  • Contract cleaning companies: Clean offices and buildings, often evenings, which can fit around another job or childcare.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

Before You Start: No diploma or experience required. Must be able to bend, lift, push a loaded cart, and stay on your feet. Reliability matters more than anything else on your application.

Tip: Most of these jobs need no school, so the cost of starting is not tuition — it is the things you need in the first month. Non-slip shoes, work clothes or a uniform, a way to get to work, and getting through the two or three weeks before the first paycheck arrives. Ask the employer first: many will supply a uniform, some give a shoe allowance, and some pay weekly rather than every two weeks, which matters a lot at the start. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like work clothes, boots, tools, or getting to work. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you start rather than after.

5Certification & Licensing

No license or certificate is required. Nothing official is needed to start. In medical settings the employer trains you in infection control as part of the job.

Worth knowing about:

  • Bloodborne pathogen training — provided by hospitals and care homes, not something you arrange yourself
  • Chemical safety training — required by law for the products you use, and the employer provides it
  • CHEST or CSCT certifications — optional industry certificates that help if you want to move into supervising

The Exams: None required to start work.

Cost: Nothing to start. Employers provide required safety training.

Keeping It Current: Employer-provided safety training is usually repeated each year.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Housekeeping Cleaner Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $14.19–$14.76/hour ($29,520–$30,700/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $16.85/hour ($35,050/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $18.07–$21.47/hour ($37,580–$44,650/year)

What that means: Hospitals and care homes generally pay more than hotels and offer steadier hours. The top of this range is usually medical settings, supervisors, or people with years in one place. Hotel work can be seasonal, so ask whether hours drop in winter.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 3,760 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 3,880 (about 3% growth). Expect about 550 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is growing slowly.

Outlook: Steady. About 550 openings a year in North Dakota. Hospitals and care homes are the more reliable end of it.

Who Hires in ND: Hotels and motels, hospitals, nursing homes and assisted living, cleaning contractors, casinos, and colleges.

Work Setting: Indoors, mostly working alone through a list of rooms. Hotels need weekend and holiday cover. Contract office cleaning is often evenings.

Physical Demands: High. Bending, kneeling, reaching overhead, pushing a loaded cart, lifting mattresses and linen bundles. Hard on the back, knees, and shoulders over time.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 37-2012); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Room Attendant — Where most people start.

Path 2: Houseperson or Floor Tech — Heavier equipment, floors and carpets, often better paid.

Path 3: Housekeeping Supervisor — Inspect rooms, assign lists, train new staff.

Path 4: Executive Housekeeper — Run the whole department, manage staff and ordering.

Path 5: Hospital Environmental Services — Same skills, medical setting, usually better pay and hours.

Path 6: Janitor and Cleaner — Buildings rather than rooms — median about $1.30 an hour higher.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • Pays more than fast food or cashier work
  • No school or experience needed
  • You mostly work on your own
  • You can see the result of your work
  • Hospitals and care homes pay better and are steadier
  • Evening contract work can fit around other commitments

What's Hard:

  • Hard on your back, knees, and shoulders
  • A set number of minutes per room, and the clock does not stop
  • Weekends and holidays in hotels
  • Hotel hours can drop off-season
  • People often act as though you are not there
  • Exposure to cleaning chemicals and to other people’s messes

Be Honest: Can you work steadily against a clock without someone watching you? Can your body take bending and lifting all day, for years? Are you all right working mostly alone?
