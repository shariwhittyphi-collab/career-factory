Cashier Career Assessment

Easy to get hired, and worth knowing where it leads

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

Cashiers take payment, handle cash and cards, and are usually the last person a customer deals with.

About 1,730 cashier positions open in North Dakota each year. This page is honest about the trend, and about where this job can take you.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a Cashier?

You ring up purchases, take payment, give correct change, handle returns and coupons, and answer whatever you get asked. Most stores train you in a few days. When a line builds and the register is short and a customer is angry about a price, you are the one standing there.

The Reality: Stop for a second and picture a store where nobody is on the registers. Nothing leaves the building. The whole operation ends at your counter. That is worth remembering when people treat the job as though it barely exists. You will stand in one place for most of a shift, be blamed for prices you did not set, and be expected to stay pleasant through all of it.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Ring up purchases accurately
  • Take cash, cards, checks, and benefit payments
  • Give correct change without a calculator
  • Handle returns, exchanges, and coupons
  • Balance your drawer at the end of a shift
  • Check ID for age-restricted items
  • Stay pleasant with every customer, including the difficult ones
  • Keep your area stocked and tidy between customers

You are the last impression the store makes, and stores know it.

3A Day in the Life

9:00 AM — Clock in, count your drawer, check the promotions for today.

9:30 AM — Steady morning customers. Two coupon questions and a return.

11:30 AM — Lunch rush. Line six deep. Keep it moving and keep it accurate.

1:00 PM — Break.

1:30 PM — Slower. Face up the shelves near the front and restock bags.

3:00 PM — Card declines and a customer upset about a shelf price.

5:00 PM — Evening rush.

6:00 PM — Count the drawer down. It balances.

Steady, public-facing work with two predictable busy stretches.

4Training in North Dakota

No school needed. Trained on the register in a few days. You do not need a diploma or experience. Training is on the job, usually shadowing someone for a shift or two before you take your own register.

Training Pathways:

  • Walk-in or online application: Grocery, convenience, and retail hire year-round and more heavily before the holidays.
  • Seasonal to permanent: Holiday hiring is a genuine way in. Stores keep the people who show up reliably.
  • Moving up: Head cashier, customer service desk, and department roles are usually filled from the front end.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

Before You Start: No diploma or experience required at most employers. You must be able to stand for a full shift and be accurate with money. Some positions selling alcohol or tobacco require you to be 18 or 21.

Tip: Most of these jobs need no school, so the cost of starting is not tuition — it is the things you need in the first month. Non-slip shoes, work clothes or a uniform, a way to get to work, and getting through the two or three weeks before the first paycheck arrives. Ask the employer first: many will supply a uniform, some give a shoe allowance, and some pay weekly rather than every two weeks, which matters a lot at the start. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like work clothes, boots, tools, or getting to work. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you start rather than after.

5Certification & Licensing

No license or certificate is required. Nothing official is needed. Stores that sell alcohol or tobacco will train you on the ID rules, and that training is theirs to provide.

Training your employer should give you:

  • Age-restricted sales training — required if you sell alcohol or tobacco; the employer provides it
  • Point-of-sale system training — every chain uses a different register system
  • Cash handling and loss prevention — how to handle shortages, counterfeit bills, and refunds

The Exams: None required to start work.

Cost: Nothing. If an employer asks you to pay for required training, ask why.

Keeping It Current: Age-restricted sales training is often repeated each year.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Cashier Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $13.40–$14.25/hour ($27,870–$29,630/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $15.12/hour ($31,460/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $17.35–$17.95/hour ($36,090–$37,330/year)

What that means: The range here is narrow — there is not much room to grow inside the job itself. Head cashiers and customer service desk staff sit near the top. The real raise comes from moving into a different role, which is why the next section matters more on this page than most.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 9,430 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 9,070 (a decline of about 4%). Expect about 1730 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is declining.

Outlook: Two true things at once. The number of cashier jobs in North Dakota is expected to fall about 4% by 2034, mostly because of self-checkout. And about 1,730 positions still open every year, because people move on and have to be replaced. It stays easy to get hired. It is not a job to plan ten years around.

Who Hires in ND: Grocery stores, convenience stores and gas stations, big-box retail, pharmacies, hardware stores, and restaurants.

Work Setting: Indoors at a fixed station. Evenings, weekends, and holidays are normal. Standing in one place for most of the shift.

Physical Demands: Moderate. Standing in one spot for hours, repetitive scanning and reaching, some lifting. Standing still is harder on the feet and back than moving around.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 41-2011); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Cashier — Where most people start.

Path 2: Head Cashier — Handle overrides, breaks, and problem customers.

Path 3: Customer Service Desk — Returns, money orders, more responsibility, better pay.

Path 4: Department Associate — Move onto the floor — retail sales pays about $2.20 an hour more.

Path 5: Shift Supervisor — Run the front end. Usually filled from within.

Path 6: Store Management — Most retail managers started on a register.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • Hired quickly, often within a week or two
  • No school, diploma, or experience needed
  • Every town in North Dakota has these jobs
  • Regular indoor work with a predictable station
  • A genuine door into retail, where the floor and the desk pay more
  • You get good at people fast

What's Hard:

  • Pay range is narrow with little room to grow in the job
  • Standing in one spot for a whole shift
  • Blamed for prices and policies you do not control
  • Evenings, weekends, and holidays
  • The number of these jobs in ND is shrinking
  • Being pleasant on demand, all shift

Be Honest: Are you accurate with money when you are being rushed? Can you stay pleasant with someone who is angry at you about something you did not do? Can you stand in one place for a whole shift?
