Psychiatric Technician Career Assessment

Supporting the daily care of people in mental health treatment

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

On a mental health unit, technicians spend more time with patients than almost anyone else on the team.

This job mixes caregiving, observation, and calm communication. Explore what it feels like before deciding whether it interests you.

Go at your pace. The short questions at the end are for reflection. There are no right or wrong answers.

2What is a Psychiatric Technician?

You care for people who are getting treatment for mental illness or developmental disabilities. You work under nurses and doctors as part of a team.

  • Help patients with daily care and activities.
  • Watch for changes in mood or behavior and report them.
  • Take vital signs.
  • Encourage social, work, and recreation skills.
  • Help lead group activities as directed.
  • Help keep everyone safe using approved calming techniques.

The real picture: Job titles vary. You may see mental health technician, behavioral health technician, or direct care associate.

3A Day in the Life

One possible day: Every unit runs on its own schedule.

  • Start of shift: Hear a report on each patient from the last shift.
  • Morning: Help with breakfast and check vital signs.
  • Midday: Help lead a group activity.
  • Afternoon: Spend time talking with patients and watching for changes.
  • End of shift: Write notes and report to the nurse.

Most of the day is steady and caring. Sometimes a patient becomes very upset, and the team works together to keep everyone safe.

4How You Learn the Job

Paths vary. Some employers hire people with a high school diploma and train them; others want a certificate or college classes.

  • North Dakota program: Williston State College offers a psychiatric and mental health services technician certificate and associate degree.
  • On-the-job training: Employers teach safety, de-escalation, and their own procedures.
  • Start nearby: Certified nurse aide or direct support professional jobs can lead to this work.

Paying for training:

  1. Start with financial aid, scholarships, and grants. The school's financial aid office can help.
  2. Ask employers whether they help pay for training.
  3. Ask for the full cost, including books, fees, travel, and a computer.
  4. Ask your career counselor about other help, such as WIOA. WIOA is a federal program that can help some people pay for job training. Ask before you enroll.

Employer help with school may come with a promise to keep working there. Read the terms first.

5Training and Employer Requirements

We did not find a North Dakota license for psychiatric technicians. Requirements depend on the employer. Ask each one what you need.

  • A high school diploma or GED, and sometimes a certificate.
  • CPR certification and de-escalation training.
  • A background check.
  • Willingness to work shifts, including nights and weekends.

Before you begin: Ask what safety training you get and how staff support each other after a hard shift.

Ask about written instructions, an accessible workstation, extra practice time, or other support that helps you learn and do the work well.

6Pay and Job Outlook

North Dakota psychiatric technician pay — May 2025:

  • Middle pay: $19.21 an hour ($39,960 a year). Half of workers earned more and half earned less.
  • Half of workers earned between $18.90 and $22.39 an hour.
  • 1 in 10 earned less than $18.44 an hour, and 1 in 10 earned more than $26.81.
  • These numbers show what workers earned. They are not a promise of what a new worker will start at.
  • North Dakota had about 210 of these jobs in 2024. The state projects about 240 in 2034, a change of +16%. That works out to about 20 openings a year, mostly from people who retire or change jobs.

Where people work: Hospitals with behavioral health units, the State Hospital and other state facilities, residential treatment centers, and group homes.

Work setting: Hospital units and treatment facilities, with shifts around the clock.

Compare an offer: Ask about base pay, shift pay, benefits, safety training, and help paying for nursing school.

7Where This Can Lead

  • Lead technician: Train new staff.
  • Nursing: Many technicians go on to become LPNs or RNs.
  • Counseling or social work: With more college.
  • Activities or recreation therapy: Focus on groups and activities.

Ask people on your unit which paths are open.

8The Real Talk

Things you might like:

  • Making a real difference in someone’s hardest days.
  • Teamwork with nurses and therapists.
  • A way into healthcare.
  • Learning a lot about mental health.

Things to ask about:

  • Emotional stress.
  • The risk of being hurt when a patient is very upset.
  • Nights, weekends, and holidays.
  • Pay that starts lower than some healthcare jobs.

North Dakota projects about 16% growth in these jobs from 2024 to 2034, with about 20 openings a year.

A question for yourself: Can you stay calm and kind with someone who is having a very hard day?
