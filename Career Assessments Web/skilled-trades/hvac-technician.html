HVAC Technician Career Assessment

Heating, cooling, and refrigeration

Section 1 of 11

1Welcome

HVAC technicians install and repair heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration systems.

In North Dakota, keeping the heat on in winter is serious business. You will explore whether this trade fits you.

This takes about 15 minutes.

2What is a HVAC Technician?

An HVAC (heating, ventilation, and air conditioning) technician installs furnaces, boilers, air conditioners, heat pumps, and refrigeration equipment, and fixes them when they break. They work with electrical controls, gas lines, and refrigerants.

The Reality: When it is -20°F and a furnace quits, families are counting on you. Winter brings long days and emergency calls. You will work in attics, basements, rooftops, and crawl spaces.

Your Responsibilities:

  • Install furnaces, AC units, and heat pumps
  • Troubleshoot heating and cooling problems
  • Work with electrical controls and thermostats
  • Handle refrigerants safely (EPA certified)
  • Do seasonal maintenance
  • Repair commercial refrigeration (like grocery coolers)
  • Explain repairs to customers
  • Drive between service calls

You keep people warm, cool, and safe.

3A Day in the Life

7:00 AM — January morning. Three "no heat" calls are waiting.

7:45 AM — First home: the furnace igniter is bad. Replace it. Heat is back on.

9:30 AM — Second call: a clogged filter and dirty flame sensor. Clean and test.

11:30 AM — Commercial call: a restaurant cooler is warm. Find a refrigerant leak.

1:00 PM — Late lunch in the van.

2:00 PM — Install a new furnace with your crew.

6:00 PM — On call tonight in case another furnace fails.

Busy, hands-on service work, especially in winter.

4Training in North Dakota

No statewide ND license, but some cities require one. Most techs complete a 1–2 year program or a registered apprenticeship. Federal EPA certification is required to handle refrigerants.

Training Pathways:

  • College certificate or AAS (1–2 years): Heating, cooling, refrigeration, and electrical controls.
  • Registered apprenticeship: Several ND HVAC companies sponsor paid apprenticeships.
  • Helper to technician: Some companies train helpers on the job.

Where to Train in North Dakota:

  • ND State College of Science (Wahpeton) — HVAC and Refrigeration Technology, AAS (69 credits) or certificate
  • Bismarck State College (Bismarck) — Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning, AAS or certificate; students take the EPA refrigerant exam
  • ND HVAC contractor apprenticeships (for example in Fargo, West Fargo, Grand Forks, and Wahpeton)

Before You Start: High school diploma or GED. Mechanical and basic electrical skills. A valid driver's license for service calls.

Tip: Start with financial aid, scholarships, and any help an employer offers — that is where the money starts. After that, ask a career counselor or Job Service North Dakota whether WIOA or other programs can help with what is left over, like training, tools, work boots, or exam fees. What can be paid for depends on the program and on your own situation, so ask before you sign up rather than after. Registered apprenticeships let you earn a paycheck while you learn — see jobsnd.com/job-seeker/apprenticeships for ND programs.

5Certification & Licensing

EPA certification is required. Federal law requires EPA Section 608 certification to work with refrigerants. Some ND cities (like Fargo) require a local mechanical license.

What You Need:

  • EPA Section 608 — Core plus Type I, II, III, or Universal
  • City licenses — for example, Fargo requires a mechanical license (journeyman after 6,000 hours)
  • ND contractor license — needed to run your own business on jobs over $4,000
  • NATE certification — optional, valued by employers

The Exams: The EPA 608 test is multiple choice. City exams vary.

Cost: EPA 608 tests are usually low cost. Fargo journeyman exam about $40. Prices change.

Keeping It Current: EPA 608 certification does not expire. City and contractor licenses must be renewed.

6Salary & Job Outlook

North Dakota Heating, Air Conditioning, and Refrigeration Mechanic Pay (May 2025):

  • Starting out (bottom 10–25%): $22.41–$27.29/hour ($46,610–$56,770/year)
  • Middle (median — half earn more, half earn less): $35.81/hour ($74,490/year)
  • Experienced (top 25–10%): $37.94–$45.22/hour ($78,920–$94,060/year)

What that means: ND pay is well above the national median. Winter overtime and on-call pay can add more.

Jobs in North Dakota: About 1,170 people do this work in ND today. By 2034, that should be about 1,310 (about 12% growth). Expect about 120 job openings every year in ND from growth and people retiring or changing jobs. Across the U.S., the change is about 8% growth — much faster than average.

Outlook: Very good, with about 120 openings a year in ND. Heating is essential here, and new energy-efficient systems keep techs busy.

Who Hires in ND: HVAC contractors, building maintenance departments, hospitals, schools, and refrigeration companies.

Work Setting: Homes, businesses, rooftops, attics, and basements. Driving between calls. Winter on-call is common.

Physical Demands: High. Lifting equipment, climbing, crawling in tight spaces, and working outside or on rooftops in cold weather.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, North Dakota, May 2025 (SOC 49-9021); North Dakota and U.S. long-term employment projections 2024–2034, via O*NET OnLine. Numbers are rounded.

7Where This Leads

Path 1: Service Technician — Specialize in diagnosing problems.

Path 2: Commercial Refrigeration Tech — Work on grocery and restaurant systems.

Path 3: Controls Technician — Work on building automation systems.

Path 4: Service Manager — Lead a team.

Path 5: HVAC Contractor — Run your own business.

Path 6: Energy Auditor — Help buildings save energy.

8The Real Talk

What's Great:

  • Essential work in ND
  • Strong ND pay
  • Many openings
  • Short training
  • Mix of mechanical and electrical
  • Can run your own business

What's Hard:

  • Long winter days and on-call
  • Cold rooftops and attics
  • Tight spaces
  • Physically demanding
  • City licensing varies

Be Honest: Are you OK with winter on-call and long days? Can you work in attics, crawl spaces, and on rooftops? Do you like both mechanical and electrical work?
